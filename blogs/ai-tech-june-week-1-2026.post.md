> Nineteen stories this week, and the throughline is unmistakable: **the AI race has
> moved from models to money, machines, and megawatts.** Smarter models are now assumed;
> the contest is over who controls the chips, the capital, the power, and the rules.

## The capital arms race went public

The week's defining story was money at unprecedented scale. **Anthropic filed confidentially for an IPO** at a near-$1 trillion valuation, just days after closing a $65 billion round that valued it at $965 billion — setting up a public-market showdown with OpenAI, which is expected to follow. **Alphabet moved to raise up to $80 billion** in equity purely to fund AI infrastructure, a striking signal that even the most cash-rich companies now need outside capital to stay in the game. Funding flowed to the picks-and-shovels layer too: **DriveNets raised $410 million** for AI data-center networking, **Semble raised $35 million** from a16z to automate small-business operations, and **Sekai raised $20 million** for prompt-built mini apps.

## Nvidia pushed AI from the data center into the home

At Computex in Taipei, **Jensen Huang declared Nvidia is "reinventing the personal computer,"** unveiling the RTX Spark superchip that fuses CPU and GPU to run AI agents locally on Windows laptops and desktops shipping this fall with Microsoft and Dell. It's a direct shot at Intel and AMD's traditional CPU stronghold, and a bet that every household will eventually want an "AI supercomputer." Huang also confirmed Vera data-center CPUs are in full production and revealed an Isaac GR00T humanoid robot reference design.

## "Physical AI" became the next frontier

Beyond chatbots, the industry is pivoting to the real world. **Silicon Valley's attention shifted to physical AI** — robots and autonomous machines from Nvidia, Tesla, Figure AI and others — while **Uber chose Munich** to test a multi-agent robotaxi system on Nvidia's Drive Hyperion platform. Even forecasting fell to AI: startup **WindBorne's WeatherMesh 6** now out-predicts the world-leading European government model, thanks to feeding its own balloon-sensor data directly into a transformer.

## Washington and Brussels reached for the guardrails

Policy caught up with the technology. **President Trump signed a scaled-back executive order** inviting voluntary 30-day federal review of frontier models, focused on cybersecurity and national security rather than mandatory licensing — a framework **OpenAI's Sam Altman went to Washington to defend**, arguing against pre-release approvals. Across the Atlantic, the **EU launched a tech-sovereignty initiative** to reduce dependence on U.S. cloud and Asian chips, while **France courted more than €110 billion** in AI and data-center investment.

## The bottleneck is now physical

The infrastructure story has a hard ceiling. **U.S. data-center construction is falling behind** as grid capacity, permitting, and supply chains buckle under demand, with much of 2027's planned capacity not yet broken ground. In response, **Foxconn and Intel partnered** on full-stack AI infrastructure, and **China accelerated its shift to custom ASIC silicon** to route around U.S. export controls. **Meta**, meanwhile, stumbled on execution — repeatedly delaying its Muse Spark developer API even as it shipped a new business AI agent.

## The takeaway

June's first week showed an industry where model quality is table stakes and the real differentiation is everything *around* the model: trillion-dollar balance sheets, sovereign chip supply chains, local compute on every desk, robots in the physical world, and governments finally writing the rules. The companies that win the next phase won't just have the smartest AI — they'll have the capital, the power, and the permits to actually run it.
